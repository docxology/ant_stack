# Model Interpretation, Accounting Limits, and Proposed Validation {#discussion}

## Interpretation of the Analytical Framework

Body, Brain, and Mind emphasize different configured dimensions: contact/control operations, sparse-population/channel operations, and policy-tree or policy-budget work. This distinction is useful for designing experiments, but it is not evidence that all embodied systems occupy three universal energy regimes. Numerical conclusions are conditional on the selected base model, manifest assumptions, and complete accounting boundary shown in `Generated.md`.

### Information-Theoretic References

Shannon capacity [@shannon1948mathematical] needs bandwidth and a noise model; the present counters do not determine achieved bits per second. The earlier claim that AntBrain reaches 0.1% of capacity is not supported. Landauer's erased-bit reference [@landauer1961irreversibility] requires a compatible erasure count and temperature. The former $4.2\times10^8$ neural optimization factor is not a retained efficiency result or hardware target.

### Biological Inspiration Versus Comparison

Insect-inspired architecture motivates sparse representations and local processing. It does not demonstrate that `structured` is a biological network or that it outperforms small-world/scale-free alternatives. The implementation supports only `random` and `structured` neural counter models. The claimed measured 15–25% biological advantage, 16-fold sensory expansion at constant energy, and 60–80% event-driven savings are not retained as established outcomes. Task quality and independent energy evidence would be needed for those comparisons.

No matched biological CoT, metabolic-rate, muscle/motor, or mushroom-body-power dataset has been identified for this workflow. Therefore no biological-to-robotic improvement ratio or species-specific efficiency ceiling is inferred. Nominal platform mass and assumed joint power are engineering choices, not ant physiological measurements.

## Module-Specific Design Questions

**Body:** solver choice changes assumed operations and memory traffic. The supported `pgs`, `si`, and `ldlt` formulas do not rank numerical accuracy, guarantee a 10 ms deadline, or justify switching at 20 contacts. Fixed baseline can dominate a compute-inclusive curve without making morphology mechanically free. Terrain and duty-cycle savings require a specified physical model or data rather than fixed percentages.

**Brain:** population activity, channel traffic, and memory prices offer dimensions for sensitivity studies. The example $\rho=0.02$ is not a validated optimum. Reduced counters alone say nothing about preserved representation, learning quality, or task accuracy. Device-specific memory locality and event-driven execution should be evaluated on actual workloads.

**Mind:** the base exact tree grows with $B$ and $H_p$, while enhanced bounded policies are a distinct model with an explicit uniform budget. Sampling and hierarchical organization motivate experiments but are not demonstrated quality-preserving accelerations. No universal horizon-15 barrier is established, and conceptual symbolic processing does not eliminate physical compute.

## Accounting and Uncertainty Limits

Rows are module-isolated compute-plus-idle estimates over workload windows. The system per-decision table uses the same selected base compute models, assumed actuation/sensing, and one shared baseline. Different intervals and repeated idle allocation must not be confused with physical efficiency differences. CoT is unavailable because the supplied distance has no matched modeled locomotion interval.

Deterministic repeats and bootstrap resamples characterize supplied values, not independent device or animal variability. A degenerate interval is truthful for repeated constants. High fit scores and finite-range exponents are descriptive; they establish neither statistical power nor physical critical behavior. Earlier contact/sparsity/horizon critical exponents and hysteresis claims lack corresponding dynamical evidence and have been removed as findings.

## Research Directions and Proposed Protocols

1. **Device calibration:** measure operation/memory costs and idle rails, propagate parameter uncertainty, and validate on held-out workloads.
2. **Timing and task quality:** record latency/deadline misses and task outcomes under supported solver, sparsity, and policy-budget choices.
3. **Energy-aware planning:** compare an explicitly implemented compute-aware stopping rule against task-matched baselines, following the motivation of CEIMP [@sudhakar2020balancing].
4. **Biological integration:** acquire licensed species/caste-specific raw records and use held-out colony/individual splits; do not transplant unrelated allometric laws or neural-power estimates.
5. **Multi-agent behavior:** specify field dynamics, independent replicates, and task metrics before fitting colony-size scaling. No coordination exponent or critical colony size is established by the current module sweeps.

A future Sobol/Morris study needs declared parameter distributions and a sampling design; a power analysis needs independent units, effect/variance assumptions, and a test. These are proposed analyses, not completed validations. Integration with external ecological resources is a research interface, not evidence of an already calibrated end-to-end simulator.

## Conclusion

The contribution is a transparent analytical accounting workflow with explicit model choices and evidence boundaries. It can support implementation checks and conditional design comparisons. It does not establish biological superiority, hardware prediction accuracy, universal real-time limits, critical phenomena, independent replication, or publication readiness. Those conclusions require the measurements and task-specific evidence named above.
