# Foundational Research and Resources

These are conceptual inputs and attributed literature findings, not validation of this architecture or calibration of its analytical coefficients. Project websites are resources, not independent evidence of implemented integrations.

## Foundational Projects

- [FORMINDEX](https://github.com/docxology/FORMINDEX): bibliographic exploration that can guide source discovery; parameter choices still require primary evidence.
- [MetaInformAnt](https://github.com/docxology/metainformant): a data-integration resource, not evidence of a completed Ant Stack ingestion/calibration workflow.
- [Active Inferants, Friedman et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2021.647732): *Active Inferants: An Active Inference Framework for Ant Colony Behavior*, an in-silico colony-foraging model. Its published proof of principle is not empirical validation of the proposed Body–Brain–Mind architecture or any specific ant neurocognitive mechanism.
- [Virtual Fly Brain, Court et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2023.1076533): *Virtual Fly Brain—An interactive atlas of the Drosophila nervous system*. It supports anatomical data discovery and nomenclature; transferring fly anatomy to an ant requires a justified cross-species mapping.

## Neural and Cognitive Context

- [Wilson (2013), Early Olfactory Processing in Drosophila: Mechanisms and Principles](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-062111-150533): a review of early fly olfactory processing, not an ant-specific input-channel estimate.
- [Caron et al. (2013), Random convergence of olfactory inputs in the Drosophila mushroom body](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12063): fly mushroom-body connectivity context, not a calibrated topology in the CE counter model.
- [Seelig and Jayaraman (2015), Neural dynamics for landmark orientation and angular path integration](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature14446): fly heading-circuit context, not proof that an Ant Stack controller runs at the proposed update rate.
- [Friston et al. (2015), Active inference and epistemic value](https://doi.org/10.1080/17588928.2015.1020053): decision-theoretic foundations; expected free energy is not automatically physical energy in joules.

## Ecological Literature: Attributed Findings

- **Abundance and biomass:** Schultheiss et al. (2022), [The abundance, biomass, and distribution of ants on Earth](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2201550119), estimate approximately 20 quadrillion individuals and about 12 megatons of dry carbon, with substantial extrapolation and geographic/data gaps. Their dry-carbon biomass comparison exceeds wild birds and mammals combined; it is not a comparison of counts or wet body mass. These are literature estimates, not outputs of this repository. The paper reports more than 15,700 named species **and subspecies** in its contemporary taxonomy, not a current verified count of species alone.
- **Invasion impacts:** Tercel et al. (2023), [Non-native ants drive dramatic declines in animal community diversity: A meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1111/icad.12672), report taxon-level local abundance and richness reductions of 42.79% and 53.56% in their selected invaded versus uninvaded, relatively undisturbed sites. These are heterogeneous meta-analytic estimates, not universal effects for every species or habitat and not Ant Stack simulations.
- **Ecosystem services and disservices:** Anjos et al. (2022), [The effects of ants on pest control: a meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2022.1316), find context-dependent pest-control effects, including benefits for non-honeydew-producing pests and disservices involving honeydew producers/natural enemies. Shaded-crop results do not imply uniformly beneficial ants.
- **Bird–ant interactions:** Avilés (2024), [The evolutionary ecology of bird–ant interactions: a pervasive but under-studied connection](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2023.2023), reviews recorded interactions involving 1122 bird species and 47 ant genera. This is the scope of the review, not a complete ecological census or a modeled interaction network here.
- **Elevational diversity:** Szewczyk and McCain (2016), [A Systematic Review of Global Drivers of Ant Elevational Diversity](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0155404), report mid-elevation peaks, low plateaus, and monotonic declines, with contingent multi-driver explanations rather than one universal climate law.
- **Biogeography:** Wang et al. (2024), [Global biogeographic regions for ants have complex relationships with those for plants and tetrapods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49918-2), provides a distributional/phylogenetic regionalization. It is ecological context, not a source of neural or energy coefficients.

## Proposed Data and Tool Integration

Pose/tracking tools such as [DeepLabCut](https://www.deeplabcut.org) and [idtracker.ai](https://idtracker.ai), anatomical resources, and ecological databases can help define datasets and hypotheses. Unit handling ([Pint](https://pint.readthedocs.io/)) and messaging ([ROS 2](https://docs.ros.org/)) are possible interface tools, not claims of implemented bindings. Imported data require raw-data provenance, licenses, units, species/caste context, and independent validation before supporting manuscript claims.

Primary identities and literature scopes above were checked against publisher/DOI records or the original full texts. The former Wilson, Caron, and Seelig links were mismatched and have been replaced with their checked intended records; no unrelated DOI is used as evidence for an insect circuit. No species-specific neural population, metabolic rate, CoT, calibration accuracy, or real-time capability is inferred from this resource list.
